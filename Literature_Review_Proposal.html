AI Agents for Materials Discovery

Retrieval-augmented, property-aware and generative machine learning for inorganic crystal design

Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) have made scientific knowledge conversationally accessible, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) grounds their answers in trusted sources. Yet LLMs remain unreliable on the continuous, numerical reasoning that materials science demands, and pure RAG can retrieve and summarise facts but cannot compute a property or propose a new compound. In parallel, two specialised model families have matured: graph neural networks (GNNs) that predict material properties at a fraction of the cost of density functional theory (DFT), and diffusion-based generative models that produce stable, property-conditioned crystals. This review surveys foundation models, RAG/agent frameworks, property-prediction networks and generative models for inorganic materials, and argues that these strands remain largely siloed. The proposed project closes that gap by extending a materials RAG framework into a tool-using LLM agent that delegates numerical property prediction to dedicated ML models and crystal generation to a diffusion model, turning a literature-retrieval assistant into an end-to-end, query-driven discovery engine.

Introduction and motivation

Materials science is entering a phase of data-driven discovery. Computational materials research has long relied on first-principles methods such as density functional theory (DFT) and molecular dynamics, which are physically principled but expensive: a single DFT relaxation can take hours of CPU time, making exhaustive search of chemical space infeasible.1 Machine learning trained on computational and experimental data now supplements these simulations, predicting properties and generating designs at a fraction of the cost.1,19 Materials data is, however, demanding: it is multimodal (structures, text, spectra, images), spans electronic to macroscopic scales, must obey physical laws such as energy conservation and symmetry, and is scarce relative to the corpora that train language models.1 Such models have also been narrow, each property or modality requiring a bespoke pipeline.

The arrival of foundation models (FMs), large models pretrained on broad data that transfer across many downstream tasks, has reframed the field.35 Following the impact of the Transformer4 and its descendants BERT5 and GPT6 in natural-language processing, the materials community now asks whether similarly general models can unify reasoning over atomic structures, chemical formulae, spectra and the text of the literature.1 Two capabilities have proved especially consequential. First, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) couples an LLM to an external knowledge store so that responses are grounded in retrieved evidence rather than parametric memory alone, mitigating hallucination.7 Second, LLM agents wrap a language model in a reasoning loop that can call external tools, query databases and act on the results, allowing the model to outsource tasks it cannot perform reliably on its own.30

This last point is the crux of the project. LLMs are powerful interfaces to qualitative knowledge but are demonstrably weak at the quantitative, continuous reasoning at the heart of materials science: predicting a band gap, a formation energy or an elastic modulus to useful accuracy.31 A RAG system can find that a compound's band gap was reported as 1.6 eV; it cannot, by itself, predict the band gap of a compound that has never been measured, nor can it generate a novel crystal that should exhibit a target band gap. Those are precisely the tasks at which specialised ML such as GNN property predictors19,20 and diffusion-based crystal generators12,13 now excels. This review therefore traces a deliberate arc: from the general landscape of AI for materials, through RAG and agents, to property prediction and generative modelling, ending at the integration gap that the project will address.

The review is scoped to inorganic crystalline materials and to the four capabilities the project must combine: knowledge retrieval, agentic orchestration, property prediction, and property-conditioned generation. Following the assignment's guidance it foregrounds key ideas and representative methods rather than exhaustive coverage; a recent survey of AI for materials science1 provides the backbone, supplemented throughout with the primary literature.

Foundation models and the AI-for-materials task landscape

It is useful to begin with a map of the problems that AI is being applied to in materials science, because the project sits at the intersection of several of them. Van et al.1 organise the field into six recurring task families (Table 1); the project draws principally on T1, T3 and T4, orchestrated by an agent.

Cutting across these tasks, FMs are conventionally grouped into three categories.1 Unimodal models operate within a single modality: text (e.g. the BERT-derived MatSciBERT23) or structure (GNNs over crystal graphs). Multimodal models attempt to fuse two or more modalities, for example aligning text with molecular structure, but remain comparatively immature: most deployed models still reason in a single modality.1 LLM agents extend a base model with external knowledge and tools, and are the category the project lives in. Architecturally, the three Transformer variants recur throughout: encoder-only models (BERT-style) build representations well-suited to property prediction; decoder-only models (GPT-style) generate sequences and underpin string-based crystal generation; and encoder–decoder models support translation-like tasks.4,5

The strategic reading of this landscape is that no single model does everything well. Text models read the literature but cannot relax a structure; potentials and GNNs compute properties but cannot converse; generators propose structures but neither explain their reasoning nor guarantee stability. This division of labour is exactly why an agentic architecture, a language model that routes sub-problems to specialised tools, is the natural unifying abstraction, and why it forms the spine of the proposed system.

Retrieval-augmented generation and LLM agents for materials

Why retrieval, and why grounding matters

LLMs hold much materials knowledge in their parameters, but it is static, opaque and prone to hallucination outside the training distribution.1 RAG retrieves relevant documents at query time and conditions generation on them, so claims are traceable to sources;7 in a domain where an invented property value is worse than no answer, this grounding is a requirement, not a convenience. Grounding does not, however, fix numerical capability. Miret and Krishnan find general-purpose LLMs are not yet reliable for real-world materials discovery, especially on quantitative tasks,31 and on LLM4Mat-Bench LLMs asked to regress properties sit near a mean-predictor baseline (MAD:MAE about 1) while a task-specific GNN such as CGCNN scores several-fold higher (around 5.3 on Materials Project).43,38 This is the project's central premise: numerical work should be delegated to specialised ML, not learned in context.

RAG systems for materials knowledge

The clearest exemplar is HoneyComb,24 an LLM agent built around three components: MatSciKB, a curated knowledge base aggregating arXiv articles, Wikipedia, datasets and textbooks; ToolHub, a suite of retrieval tools for up-to-date information; and a Retriever that pulls relevant knowledge using classical and neural retrieval (BM25 and Contriever). HoneyComb is model-agnostic and measurably improves domain reasoning over a bare LLM, a template for the retrieval layer of the proposed system. Its predecessor HoneyBee25 showed that instruction-tuning an open LLM on materials text (with one model generating instructions and another verifying them) already lifts domain competence, establishing the data-curation pattern the project can reuse.

From retrieval to action: tool-using agents

The decisive step beyond retrieval is tool use. ChemCrow30 equips an LLM with eighteen chemistry tools and a reason-then-act loop (the ReAct pattern39) in LangChain, performing tasks no bare LLM can. Most relevant is LLMatDesign,26 almost a blueprint for this project: from a target composition and property it proposes an elemental modification, calls ML tools (MatDeepLearn, TorchMD-Net32) to predict the result, evaluates it against the target, and iterates via Materials Project API calls. ChatMOF27 applies the same agent, toolkit and evaluator pattern to metal-organic frameworks, spanning retrieval, prediction and generation, while human-in-the-loop systems (MatAgent,28 MatPilot29) add expert oversight and, for MatPilot, robotic execution.

ChatMOF and LLMatDesign partly pre-empt the present design: ChatMOF27 already unifies retrieval, prediction and generation, and LLMatDesign26 already runs the predict-evaluate-iterate loop the project reuses, so the architectural pattern is not new. What this project adds is the particular instantiation that neither provides together: a diffusion generator for general inorganic crystals (rather than MOFs or genetic-algorithm modification), literature-grounded RAG with explicit faithfulness evaluation, and a verification and abstention layer that gates every returned candidate.

Two points follow. These agents show the value of delegating numerical work to specialised models, yet retrieval and generation are rarely unified: HoneyComb retrieves but does not generate crystals, while the diffusion generators of §5 generate but are not wrapped in a grounded, conversational interface.

Machine learning for material property prediction

Property prediction is the quantitative engine the agent will call, and the subfield where ML most clearly outperforms an LLM. The premise is simple: DFT is accurate but expensive, so learn the structure→property mapping directly from data and predict in milliseconds.1 Two model families dominate, distinguished mainly by how they represent a material.19,20

Graph neural networks over structures

Crystals and molecules are naturally graphs (atoms as nodes, bonds or proximity as edges), and geometric GNNs exploit this. SchNet21 introduced continuous-filter convolutions for rotation-invariant quantum interactions; CGCNN19 represents a periodic crystal as a multi-edge graph and is notably interpretable, attributing global properties to local environments; and MEGNet20 generalises this with atomic, bond and global-state attributes, predicting formation energies, band gaps and elastic moduli across broad chemical space when trained on Materials Project2 data. On formation energy the two are close but separable (MAE about 0.034 eV/atom for CGCNN versus 0.025 for MEGNet under the controlled Matbench splits37), so the project adopts MEGNet as its default predictor and keeps CGCNN as an interpretable cross-check. A related family, machine-learned interatomic potentials such as MACE,11 MatterSim10 and CHGNet,41 approximates DFT energies and forces and supplies the fast energy-above-hull estimates the project reuses for stability checking (§9).

Transformer- and string-based predictors

An alternative encodes a material as a string for a Transformer: ChemBERTa22 adapts RoBERTa to molecular SMILES, with related encoder-only models for catalysis and solvation. Because SMILES and SELFIES are 2D, crystal-aware variants (Matformer, ComFormer) inject lattice geometry and periodicity into the attention mechanism.1 The representation question (2D strings versus 3D graphs) recurs: 3D structure yields richer features for spatially dependent properties, which is why GNNs remain the default for inorganic crystals.

Crucially for the project, property predictors are also what make generation useful: a generator proposes a candidate, and a predictor (or DFT) scores whether it actually meets the target. Standardised benchmarks such as MoleculeNet33 and Matbench37 allow these predictors to be compared and selected on like-for-like tasks, which the project will use to choose and validate its predictor.

Generative and diffusion models for materials

If property prediction asks "what is the property of this material?", generative modelling asks the inverse and harder question: "what material has this property?" Early work used VAEs and GANs, but both are limited for crystals (GANs suffer mode collapse, VAEs give lower-fidelity samples, and neither handles periodic symmetry natively). Diffusion models displaced them through higher sample quality, stable training and an equivariant denoiser that respects crystal symmetry, jointly denoising lattice, fractional coordinates and atom types.8 Property conditioning then steers sampling toward a target, though the mechanism is model-specific. One distinction matters throughout: structure prediction fixes the composition, whereas de-novo generation leaves it free; the project needs the latter, to propose novel compositions.14,12

Graph- and diffusion-based crystal generation

G-SchNet18 pioneered equivariant 3D molecule generation. CDVAE13 was a landmark for inorganic solids, pairing periodic GNN encoder and decoder with a diffusion process to generate stable crystals and demonstrating property optimisation; its conditional extension Con-CDVAE16 samples latent variables from a target property (e.g. formation energy or band gap) and decodes a structure, the exact capability the project envisages. DiffCSP14 instead targets crystal structure prediction at fixed composition, jointly diffusing lattice and fractional coordinates, with an extension that also diffuses atom types; DiffCSP++15 adds space-group constraints.

Scaling up: MatterGen, GNoME and language-model generators

The current flagship is MatterGen,12 a diffusion model that generates stable, diverse inorganic crystals and, decisively, is fine-tuned with property labels to steer generation under constraints; it reports structures more than twice as likely to be both novel and stable as prior models such as CDVAE and far closer to the DFT minimum,12 which is why the project takes it as the baseline generator with CDVAE/Con-CDVAE as fallback (§11). A complementary, search-driven approach is GNoME,9 a screening pipeline that enumerates and filters candidates with GNN energy ensembles and DFT validation; it reported about 2.2 million structures below the convex hull, of which about 381,000 were predicted stable, though these figures have since been critiqued.47 A third route treats crystals as text: CrystaLLM17 generates CIF files by next-token prediction, and fine-tuned general LLMs can likewise emit valid crystals as text,42 a representation well suited to an LLM-centric agent.

Conditioning a generator on a target property

The three candidate generators differ in how they impose a target property, which has direct practical consequences. CDVAE13 conditions only indirectly, by gradient-ascent search in its latent space against a separately trained property predictor; Con-CDVAE16 instead learns a property-conditioned prior so a target value is supplied at sampling time; and MatterGen12 fine-tunes the base diffusion model with a property adapter and applies guidance during denoising. The project commits to MatterGen as the baseline generator because it ships ready property-conditioned checkpoints for band gap, bulk modulus, magnetic density and chemical system, removing the need to train conditioning from scratch, with Con-CDVAE and CDVAE retained as fall-backs should those checkpoints prove unsuitable.

Recurring limitation

Generative models produce syntactically valid structures that are not guaranteed to be synthesisable or even physically plausible; MatterGen itself cannot guarantee experimental feasibility, and generated structures can be physically implausible despite passing validity checks.12,1 This is exactly why the project pairs generation with a property-predictor/verification stage rather than trusting the generator's output directly; synthesizability itself can ultimately only be confirmed experimentally, as autonomous laboratories such as A-Lab34 now begin to do at scale.

Datasets, tools and infrastructure

Every model above is only as good as its data. The computational backbone is a set of DFT databases, the Materials Project2 and OQMD3 alongside NOMAD, JARVIS, the Open Catalyst sets and QM9, supplying hundreds of thousands of structures with computed properties for training and retrieval.1 A pivotal caveat is data imbalance:1 coverage skews to stable inorganic oxides at the atomic scale, while polymers, disordered phases and larger length scales are sparse. This bounds the project's claims: a system grounded on Materials-Project-like data is most trustworthy for stable inorganic crystals, which appropriately scopes the work.

Mature open-source tools make the project tractable within a year: pymatgen36, ASE and the Materials Project / OPTIMADE APIs for structures and data; matminer and RDKit for featurisation; PyTorch Geometric and DGL for the GNNs; and LangChain and LlamaIndex for the RAG and agent scaffolding.1 Reference implementations exist for the key models (CGCNN, MEGNet, CDVAE, MatterGen), so the project adapts proven components rather than rebuilding them, a deliberate de-risking choice (§11).

Synthesis: the integration gap and the case for an agent

Three conclusions emerge from the literature. First, LLMs are excellent linguistic interfaces but unreliable quantitative reasoners; grounding via RAG fixes factual reliability but not numerical capability.31,7 Second, dedicated ML now performs the two quantitative tasks the project needs, property prediction (CGCNN, MEGNet)19,20 and property-conditioned crystal generation (CDVAE, Con-CDVAE, MatterGen)13,16,12, to a useful standard. Third, agent frameworks already show that an LLM can usefully delegate to such tools, and the most integrated of these go beyond a single capability: ChatMOF27 already spans retrieval, prediction and generation, albeit for metal-organic frameworks, while several recent systems target inorganic crystals, including GenMS44, MatExpert45 and a diffusion-based MatAgent46. The surviving distinction is therefore not whether an agent can close the loop at all, but how: existing systems variously restrict the chemistry (MOFs rather than general inorganic crystals), the generator (genetic-algorithm or string-based rather than diffusion) or the grounding (tabular or knowledge-base lookup rather than literature-grounded RAG with explicit faithfulness evaluation), and few couple all three to an explicit verification and abstention layer.

The research gap

Materials RAG systems retrieve and summarise but cannot predict unmeasured properties or generate new structures; property predictors and diffusion generators can do exactly this but are not typically exposed through a grounded, conversational interface. Integrated agents do exist: ChatMOF27 closes the retrieve-predict-generate loop for metal-organic frameworks, and GenMS, MatExpert and a diffusion-based MatAgent target inorganic crystals. The contribution here is therefore bounded rather than absolute: the combination the project pursues, namely literature-grounded RAG over scientific text with faithfulness evaluation (rather than tabular or knowledge-base lookup), a diffusion generator (rather than a genetic-algorithm or string-based one), coverage of general inorganic crystals (rather than MOFs), and an explicit verification and abstention layer that gates every returned candidate, is not yet offered together by any single published system. Assembling and evaluating that specific combination is the project.

An agentic architecture is the natural way to close it because the agent need not be good at chemistry; it needs to know which tool to call and how to compose the results, exactly the competence LLMs do possess.30 The remainder of this document specifies how.

Research proposal: aims and objectives

Background in one paragraph. The project extends an existing materials-RAG framework into a tool-using LLM agent. Where current RAG systems retrieve and summarise materials knowledge, the agent will additionally (i) delegate property prediction to a dedicated ML model so it can answer quantitative questions about unmeasured materials, and (ii) delegate query-based generation to a diffusion model so it can propose novel crystals that should exhibit a requested property, each result grounded against retrieved data and verified before it is returned.

Aim

To design, build and evaluate an LLM-agent system that unifies retrieval-augmented materials knowledge, ML-based property prediction and diffusion-based crystal generation, enabling natural-language, property-targeted discovery of inorganic crystalline materials with grounded, verifiable outputs.

Objectives.

  1. O1 RAG baseline. Build the knowledge base from Materials Project and literature abstracts, implement hybrid BM25 plus dense retrieval, and characterise its limits on quantitative queries. Acceptance: on the fixed query set of O5, retrieval reaches a recall@10 of at least 80% for entities present in the KB, with quantitative-query failure modes documented.
  2. O2 Property predictor as a tool. Wrap pretrained CGCNN/MEGNet to predict band gap and formation energy, exposed as a callable tool and benchmarked on Matbench. Acceptance: on a held-out split, the wrapped predictor's MAE is within 1.2× of the published Matbench MEGNet formation-energy MAE of about 0.025 eV/atom37, confirming the wrapper preserves baseline accuracy.
  3. O3 Generative model as a tool. Expose property-conditioned crystal generation by sampling from a pretrained MatterGen checkpoint (primary), with CDVAE/Con-CDVAE as fallback. Acceptance: a sampled batch yields structures that are valid (charge/structure checks via pymatgen) and property-matched to the target within 0.5 eV for band-gap targets, scored by the independent oracle of O5, not the in-loop GNN.
  4. O4 Agent loop. Implement routing, tool-calling, a verification stage, and an iterate-if-unmet controller that re-queries tools when targets are not satisfied. Acceptance: on the O5 query set the agent routes to the correct tool in at least 90% of cases and the verification stage triggers at least one corrective iteration on under-met targets.
  5. O5 End-to-end evaluation. Evaluate against a fixed held-out benchmark of about 50 natural-language property-target queries with gold answers, reporting retrieval faithfulness, prediction error versus DFT, generation validity/novelty/property-match, and hallucination rate, with LLM-only / retrieval-only / full ablations. Acceptance: hallucination rate is measured by an LLM-judge / RAGAS-style faithfulness protocol (the RAGAS faithfulness metric50), and every property-match claim is scored by an independent oracle (DFT single-points or a held-out predictor), never the in-loop GNN; the full agent must beat both ablations on property-match and hallucination rate.

Methodology and system design

The system (Fig. 1) is an LLM orchestrator exposing three tools through a standard agent framework. Retrieval (Tool ①): a knowledge base of Materials Project records and literature abstracts is indexed with a hybrid of sparse (BM25) and dense passage retrieval,40 following the HoneyComb pattern;24 the agent cites retrieved passages so every factual claim is traceable. Prediction (Tool ②): a pretrained CGCNN/MEGNet19,20 maps a structure (or a retrieved/generated candidate) to a property estimate in milliseconds, supplying the numerical capability the LLM lacks. Generation (Tool ③): the primary generator is MatterGen,12 sampled from a pretrained property-conditioned checkpoint to draw candidate crystals for a requested property, with Con-CDVAE16 and CDVAE13 as fall-backs. Conditioning is limited to properties for which a pretrained checkpoint exists (band gap, bulk modulus, magnetic density, chemical system); targets outside that set require either a fall-back generator or the compositional-modification loop (§9). Outputs are Structure objects handled with pymatgen.36

The agent loop ties these together: the LLM parses the query, decides whether it needs a fact (→①), a number (→②) or a new structure (→③, then ② to score it), composes the results, and passes candidates through a verification layer that cross-checks predicted properties against retrieved/known values and applies a stability filter (using a machine-learned potential such as CHGNet41 to estimate the energy above the convex hull), optionally invoking a single DFT relaxation for the top candidate. If a generated material misses the target property, the agent iterates, re-prompting the generator or proposing a compositional modification in the manner of LLMatDesign.26 Development is incremental and tool-by-tool so that each component is independently testable, and reuses reference implementations rather than training large models from scratch, feasible within the GPU budget of a single MSci project, in contrast to the tens of thousands of GPU-hours that flagship models such as GNoME and MatterSim required.1

The tools share a single representation contract so that outputs of one are valid inputs to the next. Retrieval returns text passages plus structured records (typically a chemical formula and, where available, a Materials Project identifier); a formula or identifier is resolved to a pymatgen Structure36, which is converted to the multi-edge crystal graph consumed by the predictor, which in turn returns a scalar property estimate. The binding constraint is that structure-based GNNs such as CGCNN and MEGNet19,20 require a full 3D structure, not a bare composition: for a compound whose structure is not already in Materials Project, the structure must first be obtained, either by lookup or, for genuinely novel candidates, by generating it (a generate-then-predict ordering, Tool ③ then Tool ②). A composition-only predictor (Roost48 or CrabNet49) is retained as a fall-back when only a formula is available and no structure can be resolved or generated. Finally, the verification layer reconciles the two information sources: when a retrieved literature or DFT value exists for a candidate, it is treated as ground truth and the model prediction is reported alongside it with the discrepancy; the predictor is relied upon as the answer only where no measured value is retrievable, keeping numerical claims either grounded or explicitly flagged as estimates.

Decision points are explicit: if generation (WP5) is not yielding valid, property-matched candidates by the end of April, the project pivots to a deeper evaluation of the retrieval-plus-prediction agent (e.g. a broader property set, an ablation of retrieval strategies), which is itself a strong contribution and directly answers the project brief's primary objective.

Expected outcomes and deliverables

The project will deliver: (1) a working LLM-agent system integrating RAG, property prediction and (target) generation for inorganic crystals; (2) an evaluation comparing it against bare-LLM and retrieval-only baselines on faithfulness, prediction error, generation quality and hallucination rate; (3) a curated materials knowledge base and a reproducible codebase built on open tools; and (4) the MSci dissertation. The broader contribution is conceptual as much as engineering: a demonstration that the right division of labour (language model for orchestration and explanation, specialised ML for numbers and structures, retrieval for grounding) is a more reliable route to materials discovery than ever-larger general models alone.31
